# Exercise art — every draw command

103 drawings, one per exercise, generated by hand in a chat UI because the API
key has no image quota. Companion to `exercise-art.md`, which explains where the
files go and why they exist; this file is just the commands.

**Two incompatible styles are now in the folder — see "The style fork" below
before generating more.**

**56 done, 47 to go — 12 sheets.** Four exercises per image, sliced apart
afterwards. A 2048px sheet gives 1024px cells against a 512px target, so nothing
is lost to the grid, and it is four times fewer requests.

Names put the weight form in trailing parentheses — `Bench Press (Dumbbell)`,
not `Dumbbell Bench Press`. That is how the database reads, and the filenames are
derived from it, so the two cannot drift.

**You do not need to name or save files in any particular way.** Hand over the
sheets in any order; each one is identified by what it depicts.

## 1. Paste this once, at the start of the conversation

> You are going to draw a series of exercise illustrations for a workout app.
> Every image must share one identical house style, because they appear together
> in a list and any drift is obvious.
>
> **Style, fixed for every image in this conversation:**
> - A single stylised human figure — smooth matte grey (#B9C2CE), no face, no
>   hair, no clothing, no skin texture. Think anatomical mannequin, not a person.
> - The muscle being worked is shaded solid red-orange (#E0362A) directly on the
>   figure's body, in the anatomically correct place. It is the only saturated
>   colour in the image.
> - Equipment (barbell, dumbbell, machine, cable, bench) drawn in dark grey
>   (#4A5261) so it reads as present but never competes with the figure.
> - Background: solid very dark charcoal #0E1116, edge to edge. No gradient, no
>   floor, no shadow, no room, no gym.
> - Clean flat shading. No photorealism, no gloss, no rim light.
> - Three-quarter view, whole body in frame, captured mid-repetition at the
>   position that makes the movement recognisable.
> - Absolutely no text, letters, numbers, labels, arrows, logos or watermarks.
>
> **Every request I send will be a sheet of four exercises**, and you must return
> them as one square image divided into an even 2 × 2 grid — one exercise per
> quadrant, filling its quadrant edge to edge. No borders, no gutters, no
> dividing lines, no captions, no numbering. I will always list them in the order
> top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right, and you must place them in
> exactly that order.
>
> **Three rules about the sheet that matter more than anything else:**
> 1. The image must be **square** — equal width and height. Never widescreen.
> 2. Exactly **four** figures, never more. Do not draw a second view, a variation,
>    or an extra angle of any exercise.
> 3. One figure per quadrant, and no figure may cross a quadrant boundary.
>
> **The test that matters:** each quadrant will be displayed at 44 × 44 pixels.
> Shapes must be bold and well separated. Anything thin, fussy or fine-detailed
> disappears at that size, so simplify aggressively.
>
> Reply "ready" and then I will send the first sheet.

If a sheet comes back wrong, reply "again, but ..." in the same conversation
rather than starting over; it keeps the style anchored. To redo a single
exercise, send its one line with "Draw this one alone, as a single square image."

## What happens to the sheets

Hand them over and they get sliced, downscaled to 512, named and committed. The
slicer is `tools/art.mjs` — no dependencies, since this repo has no build step.

```
node tools/art.mjs sheet <image.png> <top-left> <top-right> <bottom-left> <bottom-right>
node tools/art.mjs sheet <image.png> --cols 3 <left> <middle> <right>
node tools/art.mjs single <image.png> <slug>
```

Slugs are given without the `.png`. Nothing is written outside
`docs/img/exercises/`.

Gemini draws a light rule between the quadrants whatever the prompt says — it has
been 8px, 10px, 12px and 20px across four sheets so far. The slicer measures it
per sheet and trims it, so there is no need to keep asking for no dividers.

## The style fork — decide before generating more

52 drawings came from Gemini and 4 from ChatGPT, and they do not belong to the
same set:

| | Gemini (52) | ChatGPT (4) |
|---|---|---|
| Rendering | flat vector, hard shapes | 3D render, soft shading, skin texture |
| Build | slim mannequin | heavily muscled bodybuilder |
| Background | charcoal `#12151C` | near-black `#020203` |
| Divider | 4–34px light rule, trimmed by the slicer | none |
| Watermark | Gemini sparkle in one corner | none |

The ChatGPT four are `shoulder-press-machine-plates`, `arnold-press-dumbbell`,
`landmine-press` and `lateral-raise`. In the exercise list they sit next to
Gemini drawings and the difference is obvious at a glance — different figure,
different lighting, and a visibly darker tile because of the background.

Both problems are real but they are not equal. The background is a one-line fix
in the slicer. The rendering is not fixable after the fact.

Worth noting: the original ask for this art was "like the Hevy images", and Hevy
uses rendered 3D figures with a highlighted muscle — which is what ChatGPT
produced and what Gemini has not. So this is a genuine choice, not a mistake:

- **Finish in Gemini** — 47 to go, one consistent set, and 4 to redo.
- **Switch to ChatGPT** — closer to the original intent, no watermark, no
  divider, but 52 to redo.

Pick one before the next batch. Mixing them is the only option that is certainly
wrong.
